\begin{abstract}
This note accompanies \emph{Optimal Sizing of Leveraged Crypto Long--Short Positions
under Kou Jump-Diffusion via Killed Moments}. It documents the empirical construction
used in the paper's showcase. The construction begins from the main paper's decomposition of
log-price changes into an expected log-return component and a zero-mean Kou residual process.
A normalized, causal exponentially weighted mean (EWM) estimates the conditional location of
the observed log-price increments, while a penalized empirical-characteristic-function (ECF)
criterion estimates only the diffusion, jump, and dependence shape of the resulting residual
increments. The forward expected log-return mean is then supplied from the endpoint EWM
forecast plus role-specific carry, or from a portfolio manager's view. Finally, the fitted
shape and supplied mean are combined to obtain the log-SDE coefficient and the expected-price
growth exponent used by the empirical analysis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Location--shape decomposition and empirical workflow}\label{sec:construction-overview}

Let $S_{i,t_n}>0$ be the observed price of asset $i$ at equally spaced dates
$t_n=n\Delta t$, let $X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}=\log(S_{i,t_n}/S_{i,0})$, and let
$\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}
:=X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}-X_{i,t_{n-1}}^{\mathrm{obs}}
=\log(S_{i,t_n}/S_{i,t_{n-1}})$ be the observed log-price increment. Here $\Delta X$
denotes a discrete increment, whereas $\diff X$ in the main text denotes a continuous-time
differential.
The superscript ``obs'' distinguishes the observed sample path from the forward process
$X_{i,t}$ in the main paper. At the population level, the observed increment is decomposed as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-location-shape}
\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}
=
\E\!\left[\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathcal F_{t_{n-1}}\right]
+\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}},
\qquad
\E\!\left[\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}\mid\mathcal F_{t_{n-1}}\right]=0.
\end{equation}
The first term is location; the law of $\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}$ supplies shape.
The conditional expectation
$\E[\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathcal F_{t_{n-1}}]$ is an estimand, not a
quantity intrinsically tied to a particular filter. Depending on the application and
information set, it may be estimated using, for example, a rolling or kernel mean, a
parametric predictive regression, a state-space filter, or an exponentially weighted mean.
In this empirical study we use the normalized causal EWM defined in Section~3, with a
one-month half-life matched to the analysis horizon. This choice specifies the historical
location estimator; it does not alter the residual Kou law or make the EWM part of the ECF
criterion. In
the forward specification of the main paper, the corresponding continuous-time identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forward-location-shape}
\diff X_{i,t}=g_i\,\diff t+\diff X_{i,t}^{\mathrm{res}},
\end{equation}
where $g_i=\E[X_{i,t}]/t$ is the externally supplied expected log-return mean. Section~2
defines the residual Kou law, Section~3 estimates the historical conditional location by EWM,
Section~4 fits the residual shape by ECF, and Section~5 constructs the parameter set used in
the empirical analysis.

\section{Zero-mean residual Kou law}\label{sec:residual-kou-law}

The residual component in \eqref{eq:population-location-shape} is modeled by the same Kou
process used in the main text, normalized to have zero expected log drift:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-mean-residual-process}
\diff X_{i,t}^{\mathrm{res}}
=-\lambda_i\bar J_i\,\diff t+\sigma_i\,\diff B_{i,t}
+\diff\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N_{i,t}}J_i^{(n)}\right),
\qquad
\E[X_{i,t}^{\mathrm{res}}]=0.
\end{equation}
Here $B_1$ and $B_2$ are standard Brownian motions satisfying
$\diff\langle B_1,B_2\rangle_t=\rho\,\diff t$. The Poisson processes $N_1,N_2$, with
respective intensities $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$, are independent of each other and of
$(B_1,B_2)$. For each asset, the jumps $J_i^{(n)}$ are independent and identically
distributed, and the two jump sequences are independent, with Kou density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-kou-density}
f_i(j)
=
\frac{p_i}{\eta_{i,+}}e^{-j/\eta_{i,+}}\one_{\{j>0\}}
+\frac{1-p_i}{\eta_{i,-}}e^{j/\eta_{i,-}}\one_{\{j<0\}}.
\end{equation}
The mean log jump and price-jump compensator are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump-means}
\bar J_i:=\E[J_i]=p_i\eta_{i,+}-(1-p_i)\eta_{i,-},
\qquad
\chi_i:=\E[e^{J_i}-1]
=\frac{p_i}{1-\eta_{i,+}}+\frac{1-p_i}{1+\eta_{i,-}}-1.
\end{equation}
Thus $-\lambda_i\bar J_i$ exactly compensates the expected compound-Poisson log jump in
\eqref{eq:zero-mean-residual-process}. No $-\tfrac12\sigma_i^2$ term is needed because this
normalization concerns the expected \emph{log} increment, and $\E[\diff B_{i,t}]=0$.

The residual law is determined by the eleven shape parameters
\[
\vartheta
=\bigl((\sigma_i,\lambda_i,p_i,\eta_{i,+},\eta_{i,-})_{i=1,2},\rho\bigr).
\]
Here $\sigma_i>0$ is diffusion volatility, $\lambda_i>0$ is jump intensity,
$p_i\in(0,1)$ is the upward-jump probability, $\eta_{i,+}>0$ and $\eta_{i,-}>0$ are the
mean positive and absolute mean negative log-jump sizes, and $|\rho|<1$ is Brownian
correlation. The jump characteristic function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump-cf}
\varphi_{J_i}(u)
=\frac{p_i}{1-iu\eta_{i,+}}+\frac{1-p_i}{1+iu\eta_{i,-}}.
\end{equation}
The price-jump compensator requires $\eta_{i,+}<1$; moments through order $K$ require
$K\eta_{i,+}<1$. The bivariate residual characteristic exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-characteristic-exponent}
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_\vartheta^{\mathrm{res}}(u,v)
&=i(-\lambda_1\bar J_1u-\lambda_2\bar J_2v)\\
&\quad-\frac12\bigl(\sigma_1^2u^2+\sigma_2^2v^2
+2\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2uv\bigr)\\
&\quad+\lambda_1\bigl(\varphi_{J_1}(u)-1\bigr)
+\lambda_2\bigl(\varphi_{J_2}(v)-1\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\section{EWM estimation and residual-increment construction}\label{sec:ewm-drift}

The conditional mean in \eqref{eq:population-location-shape} is unknown. For the empirical
horizon $T=1/12$, set $\beta=2^{-\Delta t/T}$ and estimate its annualized path by the
normalized causal EWM
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-ewm}
d_{i,n}^{\mathrm{EWM}}
:=\frac{1}{\Delta t}
\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta^{n-j}\Delta X_{i,t_j}^{\mathrm{obs}}}
     {\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta^{n-j}}.
\end{equation}
Normalizing the weights avoids dependence on an arbitrary recursive starting value. The
residual increment subtracts the lagged EWM estimate directly from the observed increment:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ewm-residual-increments}
\boxed{\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}
:=\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}
-d_{i,n-1}^{\mathrm{EWM}}\Delta t},
\qquad n=2,\ldots,N.
\end{equation}
The lag ensures that the current increment does not enter its own conditional-mean estimate.
No further sample demeaning is applied. Thus $d_{i,n-1}^{\mathrm{EWM}}\Delta t$ is the sole
estimated location removed before ECF calibration, and the exact reconstruction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:return-reconstruction}
\boxed{\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{obs}}
=d_{i,n-1}^{\mathrm{EWM}}\Delta t
+\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}}.
\end{equation}
Under a correctly specified conditional-mean filter, the residual increment has zero
conditional mean in the population. Its realized sample average need not equal zero and is
retained as sampling variation and as a diagnostic of the EWM specification. Consequently,
the zero-mean residual Kou law is a population restriction rather than a mechanically imposed
sample identity.

The same operation is a multiplicative detrending of the positive price process. Define only
the required cumulative removed location,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplicative-detrending}
C_{i,n}
:=\sum_{k=2}^{n}d_{i,k-1}^{\mathrm{EWM}}\Delta t,
\qquad C_{i,1}:=0,
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-price}
S_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}:=S_{i,t_n}e^{-C_{i,n}},
\qquad
X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}:=\log\frac{S_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}}{S_{i,0}}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:price-return-equivalence}
\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}
=X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}-X_{i,t_{n-1}}^{\mathrm{res}}
=\log\frac{S_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}}{S_{i,t_{n-1}}^{\mathrm{res}}},
\qquad
S_{i,t_n}=S_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}e^{C_{i,n}}.
\end{equation}
Thus the return transformation is reversible. Arithmetic price demeaning is not used because
it can produce negative values, depends on the price unit, and does not preserve the
exponential-L\'evy structure.

Figure~\ref{fig:price-ewm-overlay} displays this decomposition in price space. The EWM curve
is not an exponentially weighted average of price levels. It is the multiplicative location
component reconstructed from the same lagged causal log-return means removed in
\eqref{eq:ewm-residual-increments}:
$\widehat S_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{EWM}}:=S_{i,0}\exp(C_{i,n})$. Consequently, deviations of the
observed price from this curve are retained in $S_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}$ and passed to the
residual-increment calibration rather than being smoothed away.

\section{ECF estimation of residual shape}\label{sec:ecf-estimator}

The residual characteristic exponent in \eqref{eq:residual-characteristic-exponent} gives
the model characteristic function over one observation interval:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-cf}
\phi_\vartheta^{\mathrm{res}}(u,v;\Delta t)
=\exp\!\bigl(\Delta t\,\Psi_\vartheta^{\mathrm{res}}(u,v)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The residual sample contains $N_{\mathrm{res}}=N-1$ pairs
$(\Delta X_{1,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}},\Delta X_{2,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}})$, $n=2,\ldots,N$.
Its empirical characteristic function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ecf}
\widehat{\phi}_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}(u,v)
=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{res}}}\sum_{n=2}^{N}
\exp\!\bigl(i(u\,\Delta X_{1,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}
+v\,\Delta X_{2,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}})\bigr).
\end{equation}
The normalized ECF distance over the frequency grid $\mathcal G$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ecf-objective}
Q_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}^{\mathrm{ECF}}(\vartheta)
=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal G}w(u,v)
\left|\widehat\phi_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}(u,v)
-\phi_\vartheta^{\mathrm{res}}(u,v;\Delta t)\right|^2}
{\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal G}w(u,v)}.
\end{equation}
Because a short high-frequency sample leaves a pronounced intensity--jump-size ridge, the
reported fit uses transparent soft anchors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ecf-penalized-objective}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\vartheta
&=\arg\min_{\vartheta\in\mathcal V}\mathcal Q_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}(\vartheta),\\
\mathcal Q_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}(\vartheta)
&=Q_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}^{\mathrm{ECF}}(\vartheta)
+\omega_\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\log\lambda_i-\log\widetilde\lambda_i)^2\\
&\quad+\omega_\eta\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}
(\log\eta_{i,s}-\log\widetilde\eta_{i,s})^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\widetilde\lambda_i$ and $\widetilde\eta_{i,s}$ are the initializers described in
Section~7. The application uses $(\omega_\lambda,\omega_\eta)=(0.05,0.02)$; setting both to
zero recovers pure ECF matching. Crucially, neither $g_i$, $\mu_i^X$, nor $\mu_i$ is an ECF
coordinate. The ECF literature motivates this shape estimation through known characteristic
functions \citep{FeuervergerMcDunnough1981,Singleton2001,JiangKnight2002}; it does not by
itself establish that the residual increments are stationary independent Kou increments.

\section{Construction of the empirical parameter set}\label{sec:external-mean}

The ECF output $\widehat\vartheta$ supplies residual shape but no directional mean. The
endpoint of the EWM path is the annualized forecast already used in the main text:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:directional-input}
d_i^{\mathrm{EWM}}:=d_{i,N}^{\mathrm{EWM}}.
\end{equation}
The forward expected log-return mean is supplied as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:manager-view}
g_i
=\begin{cases}
d_i^{\mathrm{EWM}}+r_i^{\mathrm{role}},
&\text{benchmark},\\
d_i^{\mathrm{EWM}}+v_i+r_i^{\mathrm{role}},
&\text{portfolio-manager overlay }v_i,\\
d_i^{\mathrm{PM}}+r_i^{\mathrm{role}},
&\text{absolute portfolio-manager forecast}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here $r_i^{\mathrm{role}}$ is the lending rate for the long asset and the borrowing rate for
the short asset. Every term in \eqref{eq:manager-view} is annualized. For an ordered
long--short assignment $(L,S)$, the benchmark is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:role-adjusted-log-mean}
g_L(L,S)=d_L^{\mathrm{EWM}}+r_{\mathrm{supply},L},
\qquad
g_S(L,S)=d_S^{\mathrm{EWM}}+r_{\mathrm{borrow},S}.
\end{equation}
The borrowing rate enters positively because it is the growth rate of the short debt
liability. The selected orientation maximizes $g_L(L,S)-g_S(L,S)$.

No additional sample-mean adjustment enters $g_i$: the EWM estimate, financing, and any
manager view determine the forward mean. With this $g_i$,
\eqref{eq:forward-location-shape} becomes the main-text log-price model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forward-process}
\diff X_{i,t}
=\mu_i^X\,\diff t+\sigma_i\,\diff B_{i,t}
+\diff\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N_{i,t}}J_i^{(n)}\right),
\qquad
\mu_i^X=g_i-\lambda_i\bar J_i.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\E[X_{i,t}]/t=\mu_i^X+\lambda_i\bar J_i=g_i$. Converting from expected log
return to the expected-price growth convention of the main paper gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift-reinsertion}
\boxed{\mu_i^X=g_i-\lambda_i\bar J_i},
\qquad
\boxed{\mu_i=g_i+\frac12\sigma_i^2+\lambda_i(\chi_i-\bar J_i)},
\end{equation}
where $\E[S_{i,t}/S_{i,0}]=e^{\mu_i t}$. Equivalently, the residual shape contributes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-mu}
\mu_i^{\mathrm{res}}
:=\frac12\sigma_i^2+\lambda_i(\chi_i-\bar J_i),
\qquad
\mu_i=g_i+\mu_i^{\mathrm{res}}.
\end{equation}
The $\tfrac12\sigma_i^2$ term appears only in the conversion to expected price growth, not
in $g_i$ or $\mu_i^X$.

After calibration, the main text suppresses estimator hats. The parameter set passed to the
moment and liquidation calculations is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-parameter-set}
\left((\mu_i,\sigma_i,\lambda_i,p_i,\eta_{i,+},\eta_{i,-})_{i=1,2},\rho\right).
\end{equation}
Initial prices, the horizon, liquidation thresholds, and health-buffer choices are contract
or evaluation inputs and are supplied separately; they are not outputs of the EWM--ECF
calibration.
Thus assembly is not merely concatenation: ECF supplies the residual shape, the external
step supplies $g_i$, and \eqref{eq:drift-reinsertion} produces the $\mu_i$ required by the
moment code. The associated log-SDE coefficient is $\mu_i^X$. If a manager instead supplies
an expected-price growth exponent $\mu_i^{\mathrm{view}}$, it must first be converted to
$g_i=\mu_i^{\mathrm{view}}-\mu_i^{\mathrm{res}}$.

The liquidation-driving log-price spread remains $Z_t=X_{1,t}-X_{2,t}$. The limited
information about expected returns in short samples motivates keeping directional drift
outside the shape fit \citep{Merton1980}. A bootstrap for the full procedure should rerun
EWM estimation, residual construction, ECF optimization, orientation, and parameter
assembly.

\section{Frequency grid}\label{sec:ecf-grid}

The shape vector $\vartheta$ has the eleven free components stated above; neither
$\mu_1$ nor $\mu_2$ is an ECF coordinate.
Residual-increment magnitudes depend on the sampling interval, so fixed raw Fourier frequencies
are poorly portable. Let $s_1$, $s_2$, and $s_Z$ be IQR-based robust scales,
$s=\max\{(q_{0.75}-q_{0.25})/1.349,10^{-8}\}$, for the sample vectors
$\Delta X_1^{\mathrm{res}}$, $\Delta X_2^{\mathrm{res}}$, and
$\Delta X_1^{\mathrm{res}}-\Delta X_2^{\mathrm{res}}$. Dimensionless base
frequencies are mapped to raw frequencies by division by the corresponding scale. The
grid contains the following groups, with every point accompanied by its conjugate
$(-u,-v)$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item marginal points $(a/s_1,0)$ and $(0,a/s_2)$ for
    $a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{marg}}$;
  \item same-sign and mixed-sign joint points
    $(a/s_1,b/s_2)$ and $(a/s_1,-b/s_2)$ for
    $a,b\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{joint}}$, which are informative about $\rho$;
  \item spread points $(a/s_Z,-a/s_Z)$ for $a\in\mathcal{A}_Z$, which directly
    match the log-ratio process governing liquidation; and
  \item high-base marginal and spread points for $a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{tail}}$,
    included to retain information about jump-tail shape.
\end{itemize}
The dimensionless base nodes are
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{marg}}&=\{0.1,0.2,0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2,3,5,7.5,10\},\\
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{joint}}&=\{0.25,0.5,1,2,3,5\},\\
\mathcal{A}_{Z}&=\{0.1,0.2,0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2,3,5\},\\
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{tail}}&=\{8,12,16,20,25\}.
\end{align*}
Points whose absolute raw coordinate exceeds $5{,}000$ are discarded to avoid numerical
aliasing. Including conjugate pairs, the unfiltered construction has 236 frequency
evaluations; this cap leaves 234 for the application sample. Weights depend on dimensionless
rather than raw frequency:
\[
w(a,b)=\exp\{-0.02(a^2+b^2)\}.
\]
The ordinary spread group receives an additional factor of two. Thus the criterion
emphasizes the barrier-driving ratio without making the choice of observation interval
determine the effective frequency weights.

\section{Reparameterisation and initialisation}\label{sec:ecf-init}

\paragraph{Unconstrained reparameterisation.}
The parameter constraints stated above are enforced automatically via the
change of variables
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reparam}
\sigma_i=e^{a_i},\quad
\lambda_i=e^{b_i},\quad
p_i=\mathrm{sigmoid}(c_i),\quad
\eta_{i,+}=\eta_{i,+}^{\max}\mathrm{sigmoid}(d_i),\quad
\eta_{i,-}=e^{e_i},\quad
\rho=\tanh(f),
\end{equation}
where sigmoid$(x)=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}$.
The optimized vector is the eleven-dimensional shape coordinate
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{shape}}
=(a_1,b_1,c_1,d_1,e_1,a_2,b_2,c_2,d_2,e_2,f).
\]
There is no drift coordinate: at every objective evaluation, the residual price-growth
correction $\mu_i^{\mathrm{res}}$ for each asset is reconstructed from the candidate shape
by \eqref{eq:residual-mu}. The optimization uses
box bounds in the transformed space. For both assets the code uses
a scaled sigmoid with
\[
\eta_{i,+}^{\max}\le (1-\varepsilon)/K,
\qquad i=1,2,
\]
so that $K\eta_{i,+}<1$ is enforced for $i=1,2$, ensuring finiteness of the
exponential moments used through order $K$.

\paragraph{Threshold initialiser.}
The characteristic-function objective is non-linear and non-convex,
so the optimisation is initialised by a threshold-based decomposition
following the logic of \citet{Mancini2009} for discretely observed
jump-diffusions.
Let $q_i=\mathrm{median}(\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}})$ and let $s_i$ be the IQR robust scale.
For a frequency-aware threshold constant $c\in[3,5]$, define candidate
jump observations
\[
\mathcal{J}_i=\{n:|\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}-q_i|>c\,s_i\},
\qquad
\mathcal{C}_i=\{2,\ldots,N\}\setminus\mathcal{J}_i.
\]
Initial volatility and correlation values use the continuous subset
$\mathcal{C}_i$, while the jump-size means are anchored by peak-over-threshold
excess means and the intensities are corrected by a variance/fourth-cumulant
estimate.  Schematically:
\begin{align*}
p_i^{(0)}&=\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{i,+}|}{|\mathcal{J}_i|}
  \quad\text{clamped to the admissible probability range},\\
\eta_{i,+}^{(0)},\,\eta_{i,-}^{(0)}&=\text{POT mean-excess estimates},\\
\lambda_i^{(0)}&=\text{two-moment estimate from variance and fourth cumulant},\\
\sigma_i^{(0)}&=\mathrm{sd}(\Delta X_{i,t_n}^{\mathrm{res}}:\,n\in\mathcal{C}_i)/\sqrt{\Delta t},\\
\rho^{(0)}&=\mathrm{corr}(\Delta X_1^{\mathrm{res}},\Delta X_2^{\mathrm{res}})_{n\in\mathcal{C}_1\cap\mathcal{C}_2}.
\end{align*}
No sample-mean or drift initializer enters the optimization; zero expected log drift is
imposed through \eqref{eq:zero-mean-residual-process}, with the corresponding price-growth
correction given by \eqref{eq:residual-mu}. The threshold filter is not the final estimator.
It is a warm start that places the optimizer in a sensible region of
parameter space; see the remark in \citet{Mancini2009}.

\paragraph{Multi-start cloud.}
Because $\mathcal{Q}_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}(\tau_{\mathrm{shape}})$ may have local minima,
the optimizer is run from a
structured cloud. It contains the unperturbed initializer and six deterministic
intensity--size ridge starts with
\[
\lambda_i\mapsto c\lambda_i,\qquad
\eta_{i,\pm}\mapsto\eta_{i,\pm}/\sqrt{c},\quad
\text{for }c\in\{0.1,0.25,0.5,2,4,8\}.
\]
Remaining starts cycle through four high-jump/high-diffusion scenarios and add bounded
random perturbations to scale, probability, and correlation parameters. The
application calibration uses 30 starts (the library default is 20) and returns the bounded
L-BFGS-B minimizer with the lowest penalized objective.

\section{Identifiability and model-comparison protocol}\label{sec:ecf-ident}

The penalized shape criterion $\mathcal{Q}_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}(\vartheta)$ is non-convex.
Jump-diffusion models possess approximate ridges along directions that
preserve the compound-Poisson variance contribution
$\lambda_i\E[J_i^2]=2\lambda_i(p_i\eta_{i,+}^2+(1-p_i)\eta_{i,-}^2)$
\citep{RamezaniZeng2007}.
The diffusion volatilities $\sigma_i$ and the Brownian correlation $\rho$
are generally better identified from short samples than jump-size and intensity
parameters, which carry larger finite-sample uncertainty unless the observation
window contains many jump events. Because $\lambda_i$ is annualized, a one-year
window contains $\lambda_i$ jumps in expectation, irrespective of whether prices
are sampled hourly or daily. Finer sampling can make individual jumps easier to
separate from aggregated returns, but it does not multiply the underlying annual
Poisson count.

For descriptive fit comparisons on a common grid, compare the unpenalized component
$Q_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}^{\mathrm{ECF}}(\widehat\vartheta)$ across nested residual laws
(Gaussian, Merton, bivariate Kou), rather than the penalized values
$\mathcal{Q}_{N_{\mathrm{res}}}(\widehat\vartheta)$ whose anchors differ.
Such comparisons should be accompanied by liquidation-relevant diagnostics:
empirical versus model $Z$-tail quantiles, empirical versus model
barrier-crossing frequencies, and simulated versus observed drawdowns of $Z$.
These are more directly informative for the sizing application than
standard likelihood-based criteria.
The present empirical illustration does not carry out a formal GBM--Merton--Kou selection
exercise. Its point calibration and sizing results remain conditional on the selected Kou
model and stated empirical choices; they are not evidence of model dominance or
parameter-robust recommendations.

The repository's calibration package contains the implementation. The
synthetic calibration job tests recovery of the eleven residual-shape parameters under the
same zero-expected-log-drift restriction and produces
Figure~\ref{fig:ecf-fit}.
